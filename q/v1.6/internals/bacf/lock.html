bacf_lock

The lock of the BACF follower: the lag at which the signal repeats, acquired from like pulses and kept edge by edge.

Include

#include <q/pitch/detail/bacf_lock.hpp>

Declaration

namespace cycfi::q::detail
{
   class bacf_lock
   {
   public:

      static constexpr int          max_misses  = 2;
      static constexpr int          sweep       = 2;
      static constexpr int          multiples   = 2;
      static constexpr int          partners    = 3;
      static constexpr std::uint32_t nudge      = 3;

                           bacf_lock(
                              std::uint32_t min_lag
                            , std::uint32_t max_lag
                            , std::uint32_t window
                            , std::uint32_t limit
                           );

                           template <typename Pulses, typename Scorer>
      void                 acquire(Pulses const& pulses, Scorer const& score);

                           template <typename Edges, typename Scorer>
      void                 track(
                              Edges const& edges, std::uint32_t now
                            , Scorer const& score
                           );

      void                 drop();
      void                 reset();

      bool                 locked() const;
      float                lag() const;
      float                periodicity() const;
   };
}

Inputs

  • A scorer with fits(lag), whether a lag can be scored now, and operator()(lag), its mismatches on the full window’s scale (see the design page’s Scores). A score holds the lock if it is under limit, in mismatches; the follower sets limit to 0.15 * window, truncated.

  • Pulses and edges as lists, newest first, with size() and operator[].

The follower passes the front end’s lists and its scorer; the tests pass vectors and scripted scores. The follower calls acquire() at each edge while unlocked, with at least two pulses, and track() at each edge while locked, with the edges of that edge’s kind (not yet holding this edge).

Acquire

acquire(pulses, score) does nothing with fewer than two pulses. It anchors on the newest pulse and walks back through the others, newest first; d is the anchor’s lead minus the pulse’s:

  • d > max_lag: the search stops.

  • d < min_lag, or the pulse is not similar to the anchor (similar()): passed over.

  • score.fits(d) false: the search stops.

  • Otherwise d is scored; at most partners (3) are.

The lowest score under limit wins; between equal scores, the first found, the shorter lag. It becomes the lock: the lag d, no fraction, the score as the last mismatch, no misses. With none under limit, nothing changes, the last mismatch included. Pulses are not tested against the drop; the fit is, through the slice’s window (see bacf_slice). Where the provisional takes the first similar pulse unscored, the lock scores up to three and keeps the best.

Track

track(edges, now, score), with L the held lag (its whole samples):

  1. Nudge. tol = max(2, L * nudge / 100), in whole samples (nudge is 3). Through the edges, newest first, with d = now - edge: the search stops at the first d > L + tol; the d with the smallest |d - L|, if that is at most tol, is the lag to score (between equals, the first found). With none, L itself. This follows a bend in pitch.

  2. Fit. If score.fits(lag - sweep) or score.fits(lag + sweep) is false, it is a miss and track() ends there: the fraction and the last mismatch are unchanged.

  3. Sweep. The lags lag - sweep to lag + sweep (sweep is 2) are scored. The best is the lowest; between equals, the first, the shortest lag. If it is not at either end, the fraction is the vertex of the parabola through it and its two neighbours, 0.5 * (l - r) / (l - 2c
    r)    , clamped to plus or minus half a sample, when the denominator is positive; otherwise 0. The fraction is set here, whatever follows.

  4. Multiples. While the best score is not under limit, for k = 2 to multiples (2): if k * lag - k * sweep and k * lag + k * sweep both fit, the lags between them are scored, and any lower than the best so far replaces it, with the fraction set to 0; if either end does not fit, this step ends. A chord’s common period is a multiple of each note’s, so a lock on one note’s lag can move to the chord’s.

  5. Keep or miss. The best score becomes the last mismatch. Under limit: the best lag is the lock and the misses go to 0. Otherwise a miss.

A miss adds one to the misses; at max_misses (2) the lock drops (lag 0) and the misses go to 0. Before that, the lock holds its lag, with the fraction from this edge’s sweep. A lag that cannot be scored counts as a miss, so a lag that drifted out of range cannot hold the lock for good.

Outputs

  • lag(): while locked, the lag plus its fraction. Unlocked, the last one it held: after each acquire() and track() that leaves it locked, the held value is set to the lag plus its fraction. drop() keeps it, reset() sets it to 0.

  • periodicity(): 1 - mismatch / window, with the last mismatch recorded (1 before any score): 1 when the bits repeat exactly, 0.5 for noise.

  • drop(): unlocked, no misses; the fraction, held lag and last mismatch stay. reset(): all of it to 0.

Tests

test/pitch/detail/bacf_lock.cpp, all on scripted scores: acquire on the best of the like pulses, with dissimilar pulses passed over and the partner limit; no lock without a score under the limit; lags under the range passed over, the search stopped past it or at the first that does not fit; the nudge to an edge within 3% and the parabola’s fraction; no nudge with no edge near; twice the lag on a miss; two misses dropping the lock with the last lag held; a lag that cannot be scored counting as a miss; a hit clearing the misses; drop() and reset().
