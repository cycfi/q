= bacf_provisional

A note's first estimate of its lag, before any lock, for the
xref:bacf/lag_tracker.adoc[BACF lag tracker]. The tracker gives it out as
`provisional()`; the pitch shifter restarts a pick's read on it.

== Include

```c++
#include <q/pitch/detail/bacf_provisional.hpp>
```

== Declaration

```c++
namespace cycfi::q::detail
{
   class bacf_provisional
   {
   public:

      static constexpr float        strong      = 0.6f;
      static constexpr int          fractions   = 4;
      static constexpr float        agree       = 0.02f;
      static constexpr float        repeat      = 0.85f;

                           bacf_provisional(
                              std::uint32_t min_lag
                            , std::uint32_t max_lag
                            , float long_hint
                           );

                           template <typename Pulses>
      void                 estimate(
                              Pulses const& pulses, bacf_slice const& slice
                           );

                           template <typename Periodicity>
      void                 vet(
                              bacf_slice const& slice, std::uint32_t now
                            , Periodicity const& periodicity
                           );

      bool                 estimated() const;
      bool                 pending() const;
      float                value() const;
      void                 drop();
      void                 reset();
   };
}
```

== The estimate

The tracker calls `estimate(pulses, slice)` at each edge, leading and
trailing, while unlocked, with at least two pulses and no estimate yet. A
lock ends the attempts: if it comes first, the slice has no estimate. The
estimate is the leading-edge spacing of one pair of pulses, in whole
samples:

. **The anchor:** the newest completed pulse. If it began before the drop
  (`!slice.after(lead)`), or is not strong (`peak < strong * slice.peak()`,
  `strong` 60%; at a trailing edge the slice's peak already includes the
  anchor), there is no estimate at this edge; the next edge tries again with
  the pulse newest then.
. **The search:** the pulses before it, newest first. A span under
  `min_lag` is passed over, and so is a pulse that is not similar. The
  search stops at the first similar pulse, whose span is the estimate, at a
  pulse that began before the drop, or at a span past `max_lag`. The first
  similar pulse is taken, not the best; no bits are scored.

`bacf_period_detector::predict_period()`, the period detector's early
prediction, pairs pulses the same way with the same 60%, with two
differences: it takes the newest strong pulse in its frame as the anchor,
rather than waiting for the newest pulse to be strong, and its search is
bounded only by the frame (no drop, no lag range;
`bacf_pitch_detector::predict_frequency()` then rejects a period under the
minimum).

Two pulses are similar (`similar()`, in `bacf_bitstream.hpp`) when both
hold:

* **Height:** their peaks are within 40% of the larger. The test in
  `zero_crossing_collector`, which `predict_period()` uses, allows 20%; the
  tracker widened it to 40% for an earlier estimate (take 58's pick path).
* **Width:** their widths are within 15%
  (`zero_crossing_collector::pulse_width_diff`). A pulse's width is the
  samples from its leading edge to the first sample under 30% of its peak so
  far, or to its trailing edge if it never falls that low: how soon the
  pulse falls from its peak, not how long the signal stays above zero.

On a single note a pair forms about two periods in, before the lock: on the
Low E test note, 15 ms after the pick against 40 ms. Without a long hint
the estimate stands on this pulse test alone; its bits are not scored.

At the re-strum of the next section, the estimate was made at the trailing
edge 9.82 ms after the drop, when the slice's peak was 0.460. The latest
pulse and those before it, newest first:

|===
| Lead (sample) | Peak | Width | Against 243881

| 243881 | 0.354 | 32 | the latest, strong: 0.354 is over 0.6 x 0.460 = 0.276
| 243791 | 0.156 | 33 | not similar: heights 0.198 apart, over 40% of 0.354
| 243710 | 0.460 | 50 | not similar: heights pass (0.106 apart), widths 18 apart, over 15% of 50

The pair passes only with the wider height test: at 20%, 0.084 is over
0.2 x 0.354 = 0.071, and the two pulses are not similar. Both come from the
chord, from different notes in it; a test of height and width cannot tell
two notes' pulses apart.

== After a long lag

The estimate rests on one pair of like pulses, and two pulses can look alike
without the sound repeating at their spacing. After a sound that repeated
only at a long lag, a short estimate is suspect: the old lag says the
signal had no short repeat, and one pair of pulses is no evidence that the
new sound has one. A long last lag comes from a chord (its common period is
a multiple of each note's), a low note, a lock on a multiple of a period, or
a sound that hardly repeats at all.

The case that showed it, a strummed guitar recording shifted up 12
semitones: at a re-strum the first pair came from one note of the chord and
gave 5.03 ms, a period the chord as a whole does not repeat at. The shifter
spliced the chord every 5 ms for 42 ms, until the lock found the chord's
24.2 ms, and it was heard as a thud.

  24.2 ms (green).
* Bottom: the bits over one lag against the lag before, at each lag, 65
  ms after the drop, as the normalized mismatch count of the bitstream
  autocorrelation: 0 is a perfect match, noise 0.5, a repeat a notch. Same
  axis as above: each span ends over its lag. At the estimate's 5.03 ms,
  0.43; at the chord's 24.2 ms, 0.06. The dashed line, 0.15, is the most a
  repeat may mismatch (`repeat`, 85% matched).
* The two pulses look alike, but the signal does not repeat at their
  spacing.

The check applies when the slice's hint, the last good lag before the drop,
is over `long_hint`, 13 ms in the tracker (`0.013 * sps` samples). 13 ms is above a guitar's low E
period (12.1 ms) and below a chord's common period (24.3 ms for an E chord).
After a shorter hint, or none, the estimate stands at once: checking every
estimate cost single notes 1.3 ms of latency at +12 semitones, for estimates
that were already right.

The hint only stands in for "the sound before had no short repeat". It can
be long after a single note: in the onset corpus, 4 of 161 picks had a hint
over 13 ms, all after single notes (a low B's 16.2 ms, a low E locked at
twice its period). There the check costs a right estimate the wait of one
lag. And it can be short in a chord, if the lock held one note's lag; then
the check does not run.

After a long hint the estimate stands when either holds:

* **It agrees with the hint.** For some `k` from 1 to `fractions` (4),
  `|estimate * k / hint - 1| <= agree` (2%). A long lag is often a whole multiple of a
  shorter period in the same sound (a chord's common period of its notes'
  periods, a doubled lock of the note's own), so an estimate that divides it
  is consistent with the sound before, and passes at once. The 5.03 ms above
  does not: 24.2 / 5.03 is 4.81.
* **It repeats.** Once one lag of the slice can be scored
  (`slice.ready(estimate, now)`), the bits over that lag match the lag
  before at `repeat` (85%) or more: `periodicity(estimate)`, which the
  tracker computes as `1 - mismatch(estimate, w) / w` with `w =
  slice.one_lag(estimate)`, the latest bits (see
  xref:bacf/bitstream.adoc[`bacf_bitstream`]). A score under 85% does not
  end it: the vet runs again at every sample with the newest bits, until the
  estimate stands or the next drop. A single note does. One note of a
  chord does not: in the recording above, the chord's bitstream at a half to
  a quarter of its period matches 50 to 70%, noise 50%. The mismatch is
  read at the estimate's lag only, not at the bottom of a notch near it: in
  the figure, the notch nearest 5.03 ms bottoms at 0.29 at 4.6 ms, 20
  samples earlier, still over 0.15. One lag is the
  earliest a repeat can be scored after the drop; the full window would
  wait until 11.6 ms of the slice can be scored, the latency the estimate is
  there to save.

`vet()` runs at the end of each sample, after the sample's bit is stored,
while an estimate is `pending()`; a hint at or under `long_hint` (0
included) lets it stand at the first vet. Until it stands `value()` is 0,
and the shifter's restart waits for the lock or its latency cap; once it
stands it stays, until `drop()` or `reset()` clears it.
