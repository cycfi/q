= BACF Lag Tracker: Design

The public class is documented in the
xref:ROOT:reference/pitch/bacf_lag_tracker.adoc[reference]. This page is
about how it is built.

== The parts

`bacf_lag_tracker` holds four parts, each a class in `cycfi::q::detail`
under `q/pitch/detail/`, and calls them in a fixed order each sample.

image::bacf_lag_tracker_parts.svg[Parts of the BACF lag tracker, width=750]

* xref:bacf/bitstream.adoc[`bacf_bitstream`], the front end: the comparator
  with hysteresis, the bit history, the edges, the pulses and the sample
  clock. Its `mismatch()` is the score the other parts use: the bits that
  differ between two windows a lag apart.
* xref:bacf/slice.adoc[`bacf_slice`]: the signal since the last `drop()`, with
  its start, its hint and its strongest pulse, and the window a score at a
  lag runs over.
* xref:bacf/lock.adoc[`bacf_lock`]: the lag, acquired from like pulses,
  tracked edge by edge, tried at twice the lag on a miss, and dropped after
  two misses.
* xref:bacf/provisional.adoc[`bacf_provisional`]: a note's first estimate of
  its lag, before any lock, with its check after a long lag.
* The lock and the provisional take pulses and edges as lists, newest
  first, and scores through a callable. Their tests run on scripted inputs,
  without audio.

== Construction

`bacf_lag_tracker(min_lag, max_lag, sps, hysteresis = -45_dB, window =
11.6_ms)` sizes the parts, with `ws` the word size:

* Lags in samples: `lround(min_lag * sps)`, `lround(max_lag * sps)`.
* The window: `max(ws, lround(window * sps / ws) * ws)` bits, whole words.
* `bacf_bitstream`: the hysteresis as a linear level, a history of
  `max(window, one_lag(max_lag)) + max_lag + ws` bits, and rings of
  `edge_capacity` (256). That is the furthest a score reaches back: its
  window (the full one, or one lag of the slice, which at the longest lag
  is longer), the lag, and a word for the window's alignment. `one_lag` is
  the slice's, `max(lag, min_window)` rounded up to words.
* `bacf_slice`: the window, and a `min_window` of `uint32(0.002 * sps)`.
* `bacf_lock`: the lag range, the window, and `limit = uint32(threshold *
  window)`, `threshold` 0.15.
* `bacf_provisional`: the lag range, and `long_hint = 0.013 * sps`.

== One sample

`operator()(s)` runs the front end on the sample with the edge callback
below, then, if an estimate is `pending()`, vets it; it returns whether the
lock holds. In order:

. The front end detects an edge. At a trailing edge the pulse it ends goes
  into its pulse list first.
. The callback runs at the edge, before the edge enters its list and before
  the sample's bit enters the history:
.. at a trailing edge, the newest pulse goes to `slice.observe()`, for the
   slice's peak;
.. locked: `lock.track(edges of this kind, now, scorer)`;
.. unlocked, with two pulses or more: `provisional.estimate(pulses,
   slice)` if there is no estimate yet, then `lock.acquire(pulses,
   scorer)`.
. The front end updates the pulse in progress, stores the bit and advances
  the clock.
. If an estimate is pending, `provisional.vet(slice, now, periodicity)`,
  now with this sample's bit in the history.

The other calls: `drop(hint)` is `lock.drop()`, `slice.drop(now, hint)`
and `provisional.drop()`; `reset()` resets all four parts. `lag()`,
`locked()` and `periodicity()` are the lock's, `provisional()` is the
provisional's `value()`, and `window()` is the window in bits.

== Scores

The scorer the tracker passes the lock, at the current `now`:

* `fits(lag)`: `min_lag <= lag <= max_lag`, and `bits.has(lag, w)` with `w
  = slice.window(lag, now)`, and `slice.ready(lag, now)`.
* `operator()(lag)`: `m = bits.mismatch(lag, w)`, the same `w`; `m` itself
  when `w` is the full window, else `lround(m * window / w)`. Scaled to the
  full window, one `limit` applies to both windows.

The periodicity the provisional's vet is passed, for a lag: `1 -
bits.mismatch(lag, w) / w` with `w = slice.one_lag(lag)`, unscaled.

== Invariants

* The sample clock wraps; times are compared by their difference, never by
  their order.
* A score never reaches before the last drop: every score the lock makes
  needs `fits()`, and the vet needs `slice.ready()`, both of which hold only
  once the compared bits are all after it.
* `lag()` changes only at an edge. A drop, or the loss of the lock, leaves it
  at the last value held.
* Before any drop there is no slice: the full window and no estimate, at any
  clock value.
* The history holds the furthest any score reaches back;
  `bacf_bitstream::mismatch()` asserts it in debug builds.
* Nothing allocates after construction.

== Verification

The refactor into these parts was checked bit for bit: `lag()`, `locked()`,
`periodicity()` and `provisional()` at every sample, and the
`bacf_pitch_shifter` output at -12, +5 and +12 semitones, on all 37 files of
the onset corpus, identical before and after. Each part has its own test
under `test/pitch/detail/`; the tracker's tests in
`test/pitch/bacf_lag_tracker.cpp` are the integration tests.

== Known

* The provisional's vet runs every sample while an estimate waits, not once
  per edge, so after a long hint the cost is not flat.
