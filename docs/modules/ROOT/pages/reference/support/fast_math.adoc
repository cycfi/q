= Fast Math

include::../../common.adoc[]

== Overview

Audio DSP calls transcendental functions constantly: an oscillator needs a sine every sample, a compressor converts to and from `{decibel}` (`log10` / `pow10`) on every gain update, a soft clipper computes a `tanh` per sample. The standard-library versions are correctly rounded to the last bit, at a cost in cycles that a per-sample loop rarely needs to pay: the result is quantized, summed with noise and turned into sound. Q provides a family of *fast approximations* that trade a little accuracy for a large speed-up.

Every function comes in two tiers, distinguished by name:

* `fast_*`: the accurate tier. The error is inaudible in almost any audio use (a few parts in 100,000 for the bounded functions). Use it by default.
* `faster_*`: the cheap tier. It drops a polynomial term or two, so the error grows to the low percent range, in exchange for the fewest operations. Use it where the result feeds something forgiving, such as a modulation source or a rough envelope, and the cycles matter.

The approximations are IEEE-754 bit tricks and short polynomials, from Paul Mineiro's `fastapprox`. They take and return `float`; there are no `double` overloads.

The error ripples as the input sweeps: each approximation runs slightly high, then slightly low, of the exact value. The worst-case sizes are tabulated in <<_accuracy_and_speed>>.

WARNING: The trigonometric functions do no range reduction. `fast_sin` and `fast_cos` require `-pi \<= x \<= pi`; `fast_tan` requires `-pi/2 \<= x \<= pi/2`. Outside those ranges the polynomial diverges. Reduce the angle first, for example with a `{phase}` accumulator, which wraps by construction.

== Accuracy and Speed

Accuracy is the worst-case error over each function's working domain: inaudible for the `fast_*` tier, coarse for `faster_*`. The figures below were measured with `clang -O3` on an Apple-silicon Mac, scalar path; they shift with compiler, flags and target.

.Maximum error over the working domain
[cols="1,1,1"]
|===
| Function      | `fast_*`                       | `faster_*`

| `log2`        | ~1.5e-4 (output bits)          | ~0.05 (output bits)
| `exp`         | ~0.006% (relative)             | ~4% (relative)
| `sin`         | ~4e-5 (absolute)               | ~9e-4 (absolute)
| `tanh`        | ~3e-5 (absolute)               | ~2e-2 (absolute)
|===

Speed depends on how the calls are arranged. Independent calls pipeline, and the CPU runs many at once (throughput); chained calls each wait for the last (latency). For these functions the two differ by up to an order of magnitude: the large savings are in throughput, and inside a dependency chain the pipeline latency dominates and the saving shrinks. The build-only micro-benchmarks under `test/benchmark/` measure them on your own target.

`decibel_bench.cpp` reports both metrics for the `log10` / `pow10` pair used by `{decibel}`:

.From `decibel_bench` (ns per call; clang -O3, Apple silicon)
[cols="1,1,1,1,1"]
|===
| Function  | Metric      | std  | `fast_*` | `faster_*`

| `log10`   | throughput  | 5.8  | 0.31     | 0.16
|           | latency     | 20   | 14       | 12
| `pow10`   | throughput  | 2.5  | 0.46     | 0.21
|           | latency     | 23   | 19       | 11
|===

In throughput `fast_log10` is about 19 times cheaper than `std::log10`; in a dependency chain, about 1.4 times. `log2_bench.cpp` and `sin_bench.cpp` measure `log2` and `sin` with a dependency-bound loop, nearer the latency column: `std::sin` runs about 12 ns against roughly 2 ns for both `fast_sin` and `faster_sin`, and `std::log2` about 8 ns against 2.5 ns.

NOTE: On a modern desktop FPU the `faster_*` tier is often no quicker than `fast_*`: both compile to a handful of instructions, and the dropped polynomial term is hidden by pipeline latency. `faster_*` pulls ahead on cheaper hardware, where every multiply counts, and under vectorization. `fast_*` is faster than the libc call on every target.

```c++
namespace cycfi::q
{
   // Exponential and logarithm
   float             fast_exp(float x);
   float             faster_exp(float x);
   float             fast_log(float x);
   float             faster_log(float x);
   float             fast_log2(float x);
   float             faster_log2(float x);
   float             fast_log10(float x);
   float             faster_log10(float x);
   float             fast_pow2(float x);
   float             faster_pow2(float x);
   float             fast_pow10(float x);
   float             faster_pow10(float x);

   constexpr float   log2_10 = 3.321928094887362f;

   // Trigonometric
   float             fast_sin(float x);
   float             faster_sin(float x);
   float             fast_cos(float x);
   float             faster_cos(float x);
   float             fast_tan(float x);
   float             faster_tan(float x);

   // Hyperbolic tangent
   float             fast_tanh(float x);
   float             faster_tanh(float x);
   constexpr float   fast_rational_tanh(float x);

   // Reciprocal, division and square root
   float             fast_inverse(float val);
   float             fast_div(float a, float b);
   float             fast_sqrt(float x);

   constexpr float   fast_exp4(float x);
   constexpr float   fast_exp5(float x);
   constexpr float   fast_exp6(float x);
   constexpr float   fast_exp7(float x);
```

== Expressions

=== Notation

`x`, `a`, `b`  :: Floating point values.

=== Exponential and Logarithm

[cols="1,1,1"]
|===
| Expression           | Semantics                                 | Return Type

| `fast_exp(x)`        | Compute e raised to `x`.                  | `float`
| `faster_exp(x)`      | Compute e raised to `x`, coarse.          | `float`
| `fast_log(x)`        | Compute the natural logarithm of `x`.     | `float`
| `faster_log(x)`      | Compute the natural logarithm of `x`,
                         coarse.                                   | `float`
| `fast_log2(x)`       | Compute the base-2 logarithm of `x`.      | `float`
| `faster_log2(x)`     | Compute the base-2 logarithm of `x`,
                         coarse.                                   | `float`
| `fast_log10(x)`      | Compute the base-10 logarithm of `x`.     | `float`
| `faster_log10(x)`    | Compute the base-10 logarithm of `x`,
                         coarse.                                   | `float`
| `fast_pow2(x)`       | Compute 2 raised to `x`.                  | `float`
| `faster_pow2(x)`     | Compute 2 raised to `x`, coarse.          | `float`
| `fast_pow10(x)`      | Compute 10 raised to `x`, as
                         `fast_pow2(x * log2_10)`.                 | `float`
| `faster_pow10(x)`    | Compute 10 raised to `x`, as
                         `faster_pow2(x * log2_10)`.               | `float`
| `log2_10`            | Get the base-2 logarithm of 10.           | `float`
|===

WARNING: The logarithms require `x > 0`.

NOTE: The exponentials clamp a large negative input: `fast_exp`, `fast_pow2` and `fast_pow10` return the smallest normal `float` (about `1.2e-38`), and their `faster_` counterparts a denormal just below it. A large positive input is not clamped: past about `88` for `fast_exp`, `38` for `fast_pow10` and `128` for `fast_pow2`, and the same for their `faster_` counterparts, the result is NaN or has the wrong sign.

=== Hyperbolic Tangent

[cols="1,1,1"]
|===
| Expression                 | Semantics                               | Return Type

| `fast_tanh(x)`             | Compute the hyperbolic tangent of `x`,
                               with `x` clamped to `+/-9`.             | `float`
| `faster_tanh(x)`           | Compute the hyperbolic tangent of `x`,
                               with `x` clamped to `+/-9`, coarse.     | `float`
| `fast_rational_tanh(x)`    | Compute the hyperbolic tangent of `x`
                               (`-3 \<= x \<= 3`) by a Pade rational
                               approximation.                          | `float`
|===

NOTE: In `float`, tanh is 1 from about 9 up, so the clamp in `fast_tanh` and `faster_tanh` changes no result; it keeps the underlying exp from overflowing. `fast_rational_tanh` does not saturate: past `+/-3` its result grows past `+/-1`.

=== Reciprocal, Division and Square Root

[cols="1,1,1"]
|===
| Expression         | Semantics                                     | Return Type

| `fast_inverse(x)`  | Compute an estimate of `1/x` by negating the
                       IEEE-754 exponent.                            | `float`
| `fast_div(a, b)`   | Compute an estimate of `a/b`, as
                       `a * fast_inverse(b)`.                        | `float`
| `fast_sqrt(x)`     | Compute the square root of `x` (`x >= 0`)
                       with `std::sqrt`.                             | `float`
|===

WARNING: `fast_inverse`, and therefore `fast_div`, is within about 5% of the exact value. Use it for control-rate math where the imprecision washes out.

NOTE: `fast_sqrt` is exact: `std::sqrt` compiles to a single hardware instruction on every target Q supports.

=== Taylor Series and Random Numbers

[cols="1,1,1"]
|===
| Expression        | Semantics                                       | Return Type

| `fast_exp3(x)`    | Compute e raised to `x`, by a Taylor series
                      of order 3.                                     | `float`
| `fast_exp4(x)`    | Compute e raised to `x`, by a Taylor series
                      of order 4.                                     | `float`
| `fast_exp5(x)`    | Compute e raised to `x`, by a Taylor series
                      of order 5.                                     | `float`
| `fast_exp6(x)`    | Compute e raised to `x`, by a Taylor series
                      of order 6.                                     | `float`
| `fast_exp7(x)`    | Compute e raised to `x`, by a Taylor series
                      of order 7.                                     | `float`
| `fast_exp8(x)`    | Compute e raised to `x`, by a Taylor series
                      of order 8.                                     | `float`
| `fast_exp9(x)`    | Compute e raised to `x`, by a Taylor series
                      of order 9.                                     | `float`
| `fast_rand()`     | Get the next pseudo-random number in
                      `[0, 0x7FFF]`.                                  | `int`
|===

NOTE: The Taylor series are accurate only for small `x`; a higher order widens the usable range. `fast_rand` is a linear congruential generator with a fixed seed, held in a function-local static shared by all callers.

NOTE: `fast_rational_tanh`, `fast_exp3` to `fast_exp9` and `log2_10` are `constexpr`. The other functions rely on runtime type punning and are `inline` only.

=== Example

Apply a soft clipper, with a drive in decibels, to the input samples `in`:

```c++
#include <q/support/base.hpp>
...
namespace q = cycfi::q;

float drive;                        // in decibels
...
auto gain = q::fast_pow10(drive / 20);     <1>
for (auto& s : in)
   s = q::fast_tanh(s * gain);             <2>
```

<1> Convert the drive from decibels to a linear gain.
<2> Saturate the amplified sample with the hyperbolic tangent.
