= Resonant Filters

include::../common.adoc[]

== Overview

The resonant filters are state-variable and ladder filters whose cutoff can
change every sample, under an envelope or an LFO, while the filter stays
stable and free of zipper noise. A direct-form {biquad} swept that way
produces zipper noise and clicks (see <<_versus_the_biquad>>). The resonant
filters trade cost against accuracy and character:

* {svf}: a topology-preserving-transform (TPT) state-variable filter. Exact
  cutoff up to Nyquist and exact `Q`, with lowpass, bandpass, highpass, notch,
  peak, and allpass from one tick. The accurate, general-purpose choice.
* {basic_ladder}: an N-pole zero-delay-feedback lowpass ladder, four poles
  (24 dB/octave) by default, with resonance up to self-oscillation. Its cell,
  what the filter does to the signal, is a template parameter. Q provides
  two ladders built on it:
** {moog_ladder}: the transistor ladder's cell, with an optional `tanh`
   saturation in the feedback that adds odd harmonics.
** {ota_ladder}: a variable gain (OTA) cell that distorts asymmetrically and
   adds second harmonic, with `drive` and `asymmetry` controls.
* {chamberlin_filter}: the cheap Chamberlin state-variable filter. Cheapest to
  run, but stable only below `fs/6`, with a slight cutoff warp and approximate
  `Q`.

== Versus the Biquad

The {biquad} filters are second-order direct-form filters whose coefficients
are derived from the
https://www.w3.org/2011/audio/audio-eq-cookbook.html[RBJ cookbook] for a
*fixed*, time-invariant transfer function. They suit *static* work: EQ,
mixing, and fixed-cutoff filtering, and in particular the peaking and shelving
shapes with gain, which the resonant filters do not provide. Configured once,
a biquad is cheap and accurate.

A direct-form biquad stores past *input and output* samples, and those match
only the coefficients that produced them. When the coefficients change to
sweep the cutoff, as they do when an envelope or LFO drives a synth filter,
the stored state no longer matches the new transfer function: the output gets
zipper noise and transient clicks, and at high `Q` it can ring or briefly blow
up. Each retune also costs a `sin`, a `cos`, and several divides.

The state of a resonant filter is *integrator outputs*, with a consistent
physical meaning, so the cutoff can change *every sample* and the filter stays
stable and free of zipper noise, even at high resonance. Retuning costs a
single transcendental and at most two divides (see <<_cost>>). The {svf} also
yields several responses from one tick, and the ladders reach
self-oscillation.

Use a {biquad} for static EQ and fixed-cutoff filtering, shelving and peaking
EQ in particular, and a resonant filter when the cutoff is *modulated*: a
synth voice, an auto-wah, a filter sweep.

== Cost

Approximate cost, to help choose. *Tick* counts the multiplies in the per-sample
processing loop (adds and subtracts are not counted). *Retune* is the cost of
changing the cutoff or `Q`: it is paid only when they change, so it is free for a
static filter and per-sample for a swept one.

[cols="2,2,2,2,3", options="header"]
|===
| Filter | Response | Tick | Retune | Notes

| {chamberlin_filter}
| 12 dB/oct; lp / bp / hp / notch
| ~3 mults
| `sin`, no divide
| Cheapest. Stable only below `fs/6`; slight cutoff warp; approximate `Q`.

| {svf}
| 12 dB/oct; lp / bp / hp / notch / peak / allpass
| ~6 mults
| `tan` + 1 divide
| Exact cutoff to Nyquist and exact `Q`.

| {moog_ladder}
| 24 dB/oct lowpass with resonance
| ~10 mults linear; ~5 mults + `tanh` nonlinear
| `tan` + 2 divides
| Exact (zero-delay feedback) when linear; the nonlinear mode adds odd
  harmonics.

| {ota_ladder}
| 24 dB/oct lowpass with resonance
| ~10 mults at drive 0; ~7 mults + `tanh` above
| `tan` + 2 divides
| Exact (zero-delay feedback) at drive 0; above it, mostly second harmonic.
|===

The tick counts for the ladders are for four poles. On the linear path, a
{basic_ladder} of N poles takes 2N + 2 multiplies a sample. The nonlinear
paths run the N stages with one multiply each, plus the cell.

The retune's `sin` and `tan` are Q's fast-math approximations (see
{fast_math}), and so is the `tanh` of the nonlinear cells.

TIP: Use {chamberlin_filter} for a cheap musical sweep that needs no exact
frequency or `Q` (and keeps the cutoff below `fs/6`), {svf} for accuracy or a
response other than lowpass, and a ladder for the 24 dB/octave voice:
{moog_ladder} for odd-harmonic saturation, {ota_ladder} for second-harmonic
drive.
