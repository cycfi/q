= DX7 Design Notes

include::../../../common.adoc[]

== Overview

Design notes for the DX7 layer of {fm}: where the
{dx_patcher}'s laws come from, why they have the shape they have, the
emulations that came before, and how the voice was checked against a DX7.

== Where the laws come from

FM synthesis is John Chowning's, developed at Stanford from the late 1960s
and licensed to Yamaha in 1973. The DX7 of 1983 is what Yamaha made of it.

The DX7's laws are public work by others, and no code of theirs is used
here. The Music Synthesizer for Android project (Raph Levien) drove a real
DX7s from a MIDI rig, recorded it, and published the laws it measured: the
envelope counts and rates, the attack rule, the output levels. Those
published measurements are what stands in for the instrument here. Ken
Shirriff's reverse engineering of the OPS and EGS chips, from die
photographs, accounts for the hardware behind them: the log sine and
exponential ROMs, the algorithm ROM, and the feedback with its two sample
average.

Everything the two do not cover was measured on Dexed (Pascal Gauthier), run
as a black box. Q is under the MIT License and this layer is
clean room: no emulator's source was read or used.

=== Why the laws have the shape they have

Yamaha's engineers designed the chips, the firmware and the parameter
curves, and the tables fall out of what they had to work with.

. *No multipliers.* The chips work in logarithms. Gain and pitch are log
  values, and a multiply is an add followed by an exponential looked up in
  ROM, so every 0 to 99 parameter has to arrive in log units.
. *Tiny words and tiny ROM.* Envelopes are 12 bits and an 8 bit CPU runs the
  firmware, so a mapping is a shift, a piecewise linear segment (`L >> 1`,
  `28 + level`) or a short table.
. *Timing by clock division.* Rates are counters stepping in powers of two,
  with bit patterns for the steps in between. That is where the exponential
  rate curve comes from, and why rates pair up.
. *A two digit display.* 0 to 99 was a front panel choice, and it had to be
  squeezed into perceptual units, decibels, octaves and hertz, with the
  resolution put where the ear needs it. Hence the special tables below 20.
. *Voicing by ear.* Velocity, keyboard scaling and LFO speed were almost
  certainly tuned by listening rather than derived.

The first three are artifacts of the implementation, and in floating point
they come out as closed form laws, which is what the rate and level laws
here already are. The last two are the DX7's feel, the curves the factory
patches were voiced against, so they are kept as they were measured: a
formula where one fits, a table where none does.

=== The emulations before this one

The DX7 has been modeled for twenty five years, and each attempt sits on a
different source of truth. Native Instruments' FM7 (2001) and FM8 (2006)
came first, commercially. hexter (Sean Bolton, 2004) was the first open
model. Music Synthesizer for Android (Raph Levien) put the work on
measurement, driving a real DX7s and publishing what came back, and Dexed
(Pascal Gauthier) built on it and became the open DX7 plugin most people
use. Hardware followed, the Korg volca fm (2016), then Arturia's DX7 V
(2017). Ken Shirriff decoded the chips themselves from die photographs in
2021, ajxs annotated the firmware ROM, and VDX7 (chiaccona) runs that
firmware for a bit accurate emulation.

The line runs from measurement to silicon to firmware, each one closer to
the hardware than the last. This voice is not a point on it, for the reason
the {fm} page gives: it is a re-architecture rather than an emulation, and
this layer is where the DX7 enters it.

== Conforming without a DX7

The instrument is at one remove: Levien's measurements of a DX7s take the
place of the hardware, and a Dexed instance takes the place of what they
leave out. The reference was measured rather than listened to.

. *One mechanism at a time.* A two operator patch in both engines, with
  everything else neutral, and the harmonic amplitudes compared in decibels.
  That is how the index of 4π was fixed, and the feedback (π/2 at level 7 for
  a carrier, four times that for a modulator, matched to 0.15 dB at every
  level), the detune law (within 0.25 cents from 27.5 Hz to 3.5 kHz) and the
  velocity table.
. *Then a patch at a time,* bisected feature by feature: keyboard level
  scaling, rate scaling, transpose, the LFO's speed, delay and depths, the
  pitch envelope. Each one was measured against Dexed until the two agreed,
  and the attack times land within 1 percent.
. *Then a motif,* played through both engines and written as one stereo
  file, Dexed on the left and this voice on the right at a common gain, with
  a frame by frame RMS envelope distance to go with the listening. The
  factory patches land between 0.7 and 2.1 dB.

Two differences are deliberate. The envelope clock here is the DX7's
49,096 Hz, where Dexed runs the same law at the audio sample rate, so the
envelope times differ by that ratio. And the arithmetic is floating point,
with the envelope carried in double precision and updated every sample,
where the hardware and its emulations are fixed point.

That second one is audible, and it is measured. On the E.PIANO 1 ballad, at
the rolled chord at 21.0 s, every note of Dexed's chord opens with a
broadband burst that runs to 20 kHz, four vertical stripes in its
spectrogram. This voice puts the same onsets into the harmonics alone.
Below 12 kHz the two agree within about 1 dB throughout, before, during and
after the chord. Above that Dexed carries 21 to 24 dB more energy between 6
and 24 kHz even before the chord starts, and 27 dB more above 12 kHz through
the sustain. The grit is quantization noise, the same thing that shows in
its floor and in its feedback, so it is not the patch and not the notes.
This voice is simply cleaner, and the dirt is not worth adding back unless
the DX7's dirt is what you are after. The one place they part company for
the same reason is the chaotic feedback regime, feedback 6 or 7 with a
modulator near full level, where Dexed's loop breaks up and this one stays
periodic.

== The DX7's 32 Algorithms

The 32 algorithm charts are `dx7_routing` on the {fm_routing} page.
They are a useful set of six operator topologies whatever synth uses them, so
they sit beside `fm_routing` rather than with the DX7's own parameters.
