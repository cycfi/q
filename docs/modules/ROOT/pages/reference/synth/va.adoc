= Virtual Analog

include::../../common.adoc[]

== Overview

Virtual analog is subtractive synthesis: start with a waveform rich in
harmonics, take some away with a filter, and shape what is left with
envelopes. It is what the polyphonic synthesizers of the late 1970s and
1980s did, and the reason their parts are worth modelling is that the
circuits had character, not merely function.

Q's virtual analog parts are general building blocks that take after those
instruments without imitating any of them. Where a behaviour comes from a
real circuit, the page for it says which and why.

The harmonics were all there to begin with, and the filter takes them away until a sine is left.", link={imagesdir}/vcf_sweep.svg]

== How it sounds

Six short phrases through a voice built from these parts: two `{analog_osc}`
a few cents apart, a ladder filter with an envelope on its cutoff, and the
envelopes and LFO around them. One patch per character, voiced by ear. The
first five use `{ota_ladder}`. The last is a different animal:
`{moog_ladder}` with its saturation on, a square an octave below the two
oscillators, and a `{soft_clip}` in front of the filter, which is where the
growl of that sound comes from.

`example/va_synth` renders them all: the patches are written out in its
source, and the notes come from the MIDI files beside it, read with
`{midi_file}`.

=== The parts

[cols="1,3"]
|===
| Component | What it does

| `{analog_osc}`      | One oscillator core giving sawtooth, pulse and
                        triangle from a single phase, as a voltage
                        controlled oscillator chip does.
| `{saw_osc}`, `{pulse_osc}`, `{triangle_osc}`, `{square_osc}`
                      | The individual waveforms, band limited, for a voice
                        built on a single shape.
| `{hard_sync}`       | One oscillator restarted by another, the sync input
                        that makes a lead tear as it sweeps.
| `{basic_ladder}`    | The four-pole ladder filter, with the cell and the
                        pole count as parameters.
| `{moog_ladder}`     | That ladder with a transistor ladder's cell: odd
                        harmonics, the growl.
| `{ota_ladder}`      | The same with a variable gain cell, as the Curtis
                        chips in a Prophet-5 or an OB-Xa had: second
                        harmonic, a smoother drive.
| `{svf}`             | A state variable filter, for 12 dB per octave and
                        the other responses.
| `{adsr_envelope_gen}` | The envelopes.
| `{lfo_gen}`         | The modulation source, with delay and fade.
|===

=== What the era's circuits contribute

Three behaviours here exist because the hardware behaved that way, and each
is a control rather than an assumption:

* *The filter's cell.* A transistor ladder saturates symmetrically and adds
  odd harmonics. The Curtis CEM3320, the filter in a Prophet-5 Rev 3, an
  OB-Xa and an OB-8, uses variable gain cells that run off centre, and its
  data sheet puts the passband distortion at 0.1 percent and
  "predominantly second harmonic". `{ota_ladder}` has `drive` and
  `asymmetry` for exactly that.
* *The pole count.* Four poles is the classic 24 dB per octave voice. The
  OB-Xa put a two-pole setting on the same chip, and that switch is part of
  how those records sound, so `{basic_ladder}` takes N as a parameter.
* *The triangle's symmetry.* A CEM3340 specifies its triangle at 50 percent
  with a 45 to 55 percent range. Skewing it walks the wave toward a
  sawtooth and brings up the even harmonics, which `{triangle_osc}` exposes
  as a control.

NOTE: None of this is an emulation. Q models no particular instrument, and
nothing here is calibrated against one. What it takes from those circuits is
the shape of their behaviour, which is documented in data sheets anyone can
read.

== Include

```c++
#include <q/synth/va/analog_osc.hpp>   // the oscillator core
#include <q/synth/va/sync.hpp>         // hard sync
#include <q/fx/ladder.hpp>             // both ladder filters
```

== Example

A voice in the idiom: two oscillators a few cents apart, a sawtooth and a
pulse, through the OTA ladder with an envelope on the cutoff.

```c++
q::analog_osc a{0.4f}, b{0.5f};
q::phase_iterator pa{220_Hz, sps};
q::phase_iterator pb{q::frequency{220 * std::pow(2.0, 7.0 / 1200)}, sps};

q::ota_ladder filter{600_Hz, sps, 0.3f};
filter.drive(1.2f);

for (auto& s : buffer)
{
   auto e = env();                        // a filter envelope
   filter.cutoff(q::frequency{220 + 4000 * e * e}, sps);

   auto mix = 0.6f * a.saw(pa) + 0.4f * b.pulse(pb);
   s = filter(mix * 0.5f) * amp();
   ++pa;
   ++pb;
}
```
